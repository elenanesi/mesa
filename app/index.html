      Planning preferences

      How Mesa plans

      A clear distinction between public-health guidance, estimates from your information, and Mesa’s own planning rules.

      GuidelineEstimateMesa rule
      Estimate

      Daily calories and recipe nutrition

      Your recommended calories use the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, your age, height, weight, sex and selected activity level. It is a population equation, not a measurement of your metabolism. Recipe energy and macros are calculated from Mesa’s ingredient data and portions.

      Guideline

      What Mesa compares with public guidance

      • Fibre: 25g per day.
      • Free sugars: under 10% of daily energy.
      • Saturated fat: under 10% of daily energy.

      These are general population measures, not personalised prescriptions. Mesa shows them only where its data can calculate the measure.

      Mesa rule

      How the planner chooses meals

      Mesa fits meals to your calorie target, selected macro split, dietary restrictions, avoids, sharing choices and recipe variety. The calorie adjustments for fat loss (−325 kcal), muscle gain (+60 kcal), macro ranges, favourite boosts and the ±10% target-consistency band are Mesa settings—not clinical guidelines.

      “Higher protein” and “higher fibre, lower saturated fat” are preference nudges in the planner. They do not diagnose, treat or prevent a condition.

      What Mesa does not claim

      Mesa does not assess thyroid or skin conditions, iodine, selenium, omega-3 adequacy, low-GI suitability, micronutrient sufficiency or medical dietary needs. Ask a qualified clinician for individual advice, especially for a condition, medication, pregnancy or a therapeutic diet.

      Sources

      WHO healthy diet guidance (fibre and saturated fat) · WHO sugars guideline · Mifflin–St Jeor equation.

      Reviewed July 2026. Sources describe broad guidance; they do not validate every individual meal or outcome.
